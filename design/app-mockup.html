Seattle Grocery Deals, the app

A mockup of a dedicated iPhone app in the same newsprint style as the site. The big new ideas: your Reminders grocery list gets matched against this week's deals, and push notifications tell you when something on your list goes on sale. Real deals from the week of Oct 23. The plus buttons, list checkboxes and switches work.

How it would work

  • Reminders sync: iOS lets apps read and write Reminders lists through EventKit. In Expo, the expo-calendar module covers this on iOS (needs a development build, not Expo Go). The app reads your chosen list, matches each item to deals, and writes new items back. Apple's automatic grocery sections in Reminders may not be settable from an app, so items could land unsorted.
  • Matching: "persimmons" or "green onions" in your list is matched against deal names and the site's item keys (persimmons, rice, shrimp…). That runs on the phone against the week's JSON, so your list never leaves it.
  • Push: the Friday routine publishes the week, then a small backend job sends one digest per phone through Apple's push service (Expo's push service in front of it). Price watches and list matches have to be known to that job, so phones send it only the item keys you watch, not your full list.
  • Android: there's no Reminders equivalent with an open API, so Android would use the in-app list (Google Tasks sync could come later).

More ideas

  • Plan my trip: pick the fewest stores that cover your sale items, with a map and directions.
  • In-store mode: your list sorted by department, big text, screen stays awake.
  • Share a deal: send a deal card to a friend or group chat.
  • Apple Watch: your list on your wrist while you shop.
  • Siri: "What's on sale at 99 Ranch?" or "Add persimmons to my grocery list."
  • Spotlight a store: the editorial "show them some love" card, when you're ready.

Build order

  • 1. Expo app reading the site's weekly JSON: This week, deal detail, stores. No backend yet.
  • 2. Reminders list matching and "Add to list" (all on device).
  • 3. Backend: Postgres on Neon, an API, and a Friday push job. Amplitude for app analytics.
  • 4. Price watches, widgets, then Android with the in-app list.
